UNDERSTANDING BACKUP & DR

Backup Is Not the Same as Disaster Recovery

A strong resilience strategy considers all three—not just where your backup is stored.

Backup

Backup creates protected copies of your data and systems so they can be restored when needed.

Disaster Recovery

Disaster recovery focuses on how your systems, applications and services will be restored and made available after a major disruption.

Business Continuity

Business continuity considers how your organization continues operating while systems are unavailable or being recovered.

BACKUP SOLUTIONS

Reliable Backup for Critical Business Data

A backup strategy should provide more than a copy of your data. It should define what is protected, where backups are stored, how often they are created and how quickly they can be restored.

Local Backup

Maintain backup copies within your local environment for fast access and recovery where appropriate.

Offsite Backup

Maintain protected copies outside the primary environment to reduce the impact of site-level incidents.

Cloud Backup

Use cloud-based storage and backup services to provide additional protection and scalable retention options.

Immutable Backup

Use technologies and architectures designed to help protect backup copies from unauthorized modification or deletion.

Automated Backup

Schedule and automate backup operations to reduce manual intervention and improve consistency.

Backup Monitoring

Monitor backup jobs and failures so issues can be identified before a recovery is required.

DISASTER RECOVERY

When Something Goes Wrong, Know What Happens Next.

A disaster recovery strategy defines how critical systems will be restored after an incident and establishes the priorities, processes and technologies required to recover operations.

Recovery Planning

Identify critical systems, dependencies, recovery priorities and recovery requirements.

Recovery Objectives

Define appropriate Recovery Point Objectives (RPO) and Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) based on business requirements.

Disaster Recovery Infrastructure

Design recovery environments using appropriate on-premises, cloud or hybrid technologies.

Failover & Recovery

Establish procedures for restoring or failing over critical workloads when the primary environment is unavailable.

Disaster Recovery Testing

Test recovery procedures to validate that systems, data and processes can be recovered as expected.

Recovery Documentation

Document recovery procedures, responsibilities and escalation paths so your team knows what to do during an incident.

RECOVERY OBJECTIVES

How Fast Do You Need to Recover?

Badee can help define recovery objectives based on the business impact of downtime and data loss.

RPO — Recovery Point Objective

How much data can your business afford to lose? An RPO of one hour means the recovery strategy is designed around potentially losing no more than approximately one hour of data, subject to the actual technology and configuration.

RTO — Recovery Time Objective

How quickly does a critical system need to be restored? The required RTO helps determine the appropriate recovery architecture, technology and investment.
